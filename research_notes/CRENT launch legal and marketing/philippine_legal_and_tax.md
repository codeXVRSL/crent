**Research-method note for the report writer.** The network proxy in this session blocked direct page fetches for virtually every Philippine government and law-firm domain (dti.gov.ph, bir.gov.ph, bsp.gov.ph, privacy.gov.ph, ipophil.gov.ph, lawphil.net, sec.gov.ph, respicio.ph, divinalaw.com, cruzmarcelo.com, accralaw.com, kpmg, grantthornton, etc.). **Two premise corrections to flag prominently in the report:**
1. **RA 12063 is NOT the Freelance Workers Protection Act.** Search results show RA 12063 (approved 2024, IRR issued 28 Feb 2025) is the *Enterprise-Based Education and Training (EBET) Framework Act*. The Freelance Workers Protection Act is still a **pending bill** (passed the House on 3rd reading; several Senate versions; a new bill was reportedly filed as late as December 2025). It is not yet law as of the sources found. See Key Question 6.
2. **NPC Circular 2024-01 is a procedural-rules amendment, not the fines schedule.** The administrative-fines schedule is NPC Circular 2022-01. See Key Question 3.

## Key Question 1 — Business registration: DTI sole proprietorship vs SEC One Person Corporation; Naga City permits; BIR registration; BMBE

### Takeaway
A sole proprietorship (DTI business name + Naga City permit + BIR Form 1901) is the cheapest and fastest route (roughly PHP 2,000–3,000 in national government fees plus LGU fees), but gives the founder unlimited personal liability for the money held for creators and researchers; an SEC One Person Corporation has no statutory minimum capital, costs a few thousand pesos more, and ring-fences personal assets, which matters for a platform that holds other people's funds. BMBE registration can waive income tax for a micro business with assets of PHP 3M or less, but it is unclear from the sources whether a service platform like CRENT qualifies.

### Cited Findings

**DTI business name (sole proprietorship)**
- DTI BNRS registration fees by territorial scope: Barangay PHP 200; City/Municipality PHP 500; Regional PHP 1,000; National PHP 2,000; all plus PHP 30 documentary stamp tax. — [Orkids BNRS guide](https://orkids.ph/guides/dti-business-name-registration); [Pinoy Negosyo 2026 guide](https://pinoynegosyo.net/dti-business-registration-3912.html)
- Territorial scope "refers to the extent of the geographical area within which the pertinent business may locate its offices, stores, shops, branches ... It is not to be considered as the geographical limit in which to transact business." — [DTI BNRS FAQ](https://bnrs.dti.gov.ph/faq) (via search snippet)
- National scope "provides full name protection anywhere in the Philippines and is necessary if you intend to open branches nationwide, franchise, or build a brand you'll eventually sell. Fee reaches ₱2,030." — [Pinoy Negosyo](https://pinoynegosyo.net/dti-business-registration-3912.html)
- A business name registration is valid for five (5) years from the date of registration; late renewal carries a 50% surcharge. — [Orkids](https://orkids.ph/guides/dti-business-name-registration); [Grab TNVS guide](https://www.grab.com/ph/tnvs/tnvs-requirements/how-to-get-dti-business-name-registration/)
- The registered business name (legal name) may differ from the brand/trade name used in marketing; the brand can be separately protected by an IPOPHL trademark. — [FilipiKnow](https://filipiknow.net/dti-online-business-name-registration/); [The Business Manual](https://thebusinessmanual.ph/business-101/sme-basics/business-name-registration-online-dti/)

**SEC One Person Corporation (OPC)**
- SEC basic filing fee is 1/5 of 1% (0.002) of authorized capital stock or the subscription price, whichever is higher, but not less than PHP 2,000; plus name verification PHP 100 per name; Legal Research Fee 1% of filing fee (min PHP 10, max PHP 500); By-Laws filing fee PHP 510; Stock and Transfer Book registration PHP 360. — [Respicio, OPC fees](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/legal-and-government-fees-for-registering-a-one-person-corporation-opc-in-the-philippines) (via search snippet)
- The SEC offers a 20% discount on registration fees and up to 50% discount on filing fees for MSMEs. — [Wise, SEC registration 2025](https://wise.com/ph/blog/sec-business-registration)
- "There are no mandatory capital thresholds for OPCs unless specified by law"; an OPC "has no general statutory minimum authorized capital stock and is not subject to the ordinary minimum-subscription and minimum-paid-up-capital rules unless a special law or regulation provides otherwise." — [InCorp OPC guide 2026](https://philippines.incorp.asia/guides/how-to-set-up-one-person-corporation/); [Respicio](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/sec-registration-compliance-requirements-for-one-person-corporation)
- OneSEC (part of eSPARC, launched 2021) enables companies to register "in as fast as one minute and 14 seconds." — [Forvis Mazars on eSPARC](https://www.forvismazars.com/ph/en/insights/our-publications/doing-business-in-the-philippines-updates/sec-esparc-online-business-registration); eSPARC portal: [esparc.sec.gov.ph](https://esparc.sec.gov.ph/application/selection)

**Naga City permits**
- New business permit requirements in Naga City: Business License Application/Assessment Form; Community Tax Certificate (cedula); Barangay Business Clearance; DTI Business Name Registration (sole prop) or SEC Articles (corporation); amount of capitalization; 2x2 photo of owner; 3x4 photo of establishment façade. Additional clearances: Zoning Clearance, Sanitary Permit (Health Office), Occupancy Permit (Engineering), Fire Safety Inspection Certificate (BFP), BIR clearance, SSS and Pag-IBIG registration. — [Naga City documentary requirements (PDF)](https://www2.naga.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/BP-DOCUMENTARY-REQUIREMENTS-A4.pdf) and [Naga City service page](https://www2.naga.gov.ph/2023-city-service/business-permit-application-and-renewal/) (via search snippets)
- "All enterprises are required to secure a Business License and Mayor's Permit, and pay business taxes before the start of commercial operations"; applications go through the Business One-Stop Shop at the Naga City People's Hall, where representatives of the city's 27 barangays issue Barangay Business Clearances. — [Naga City CTO citizen's charter (PDF)](https://www2.naga.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/CC-4E-CTO-Applying-Renewing-a-Business-Permit.pdf); [PIA on Cam Sur BOSS](https://pia.gov.ph/cam-sur-lgus-open-business-one-stop-shop-2023/)

**BIR registration**
- BIR Form 1901 is for individuals engaged in business or practice of profession (self-employed, single proprietors, mixed-income earners); Form 1903 is for corporations/partnerships. — [PinoyGovGuide, Form 1901](https://pinoygovguide.com/bir/self-employed-registration); [PinoyGovGuide, Form 1903](https://pinoygovguide.com/bir/business-registration); [BIR checklist of documentary requirements 2025 (PDF)](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/CDR%20-%202025%20(1).pdf)
- The PHP 500 Annual Registration Fee was abolished by the Ease of Paying Taxes Act (RA 11976, signed 5 Jan 2024, effective 22 Jan 2024), which deleted NIRC §236(B); the remaining registration cost is PHP 30 documentary stamp tax (paid with Form 2000) for books of accounts. — [LiveInPH 2026 guide](https://liveinph.com/bir-registration-freelancer-philippines-2026); [BIR EOPT flyer (PDF)](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/flyer-eopt.pdf); [Jobbers.io](https://www.jobbers.io/bir-registration-for-filipino-freelancers-complete-step-by-step-guide-2025/)
- BIR Form 1906 (Authority to Print) is used for printed invoices; sole proprietors need BIR-printed invoices or samples of their own invoices printed by a BIR-accredited printer. — [Jobbers.io](https://www.jobbers.io/bir-registration-for-filipino-freelancers-complete-step-by-step-guide-2025/)
- Under RR 7-2024 (effective 27 April 2024, implementing EOPT) the "invoice" replaces the official receipt as the primary document for services; invoices must show seller and buyer information, quantity, unit cost, description/nature of service, and VAT rate/amount or exemption (RR 7-2024 Sec. 6(B)). — [Grant Thornton, RR 7-2024](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/lets-talk-tax/clarification-for-compliance-with-invoicing-requirements-bir-rr-no-7-2024/); [PwC "EoPT invoicing, clarified"](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/taxwise-or-otherwise/2024/eopt-invoicing-clarified.html); [MPM.ph](https://mpm.ph/how-to-register-invoice-per-rr-7-2024/)
- **E-invoicing (RR 11-2025):** mandatory electronic invoicing and electronic sales reporting applies, one year from 14 March 2025 (i.e., 14 March 2026), to taxpayers "engaged in electronic commerce (e-commerce) or internet transactions such as sale of digital goods/content/products, digital financial services, and online entertainment services," large taxpayers, and LTS taxpayers; the BIR then issued **RR 26-2025 extending the deadline to 31 December 2026**. Micro taxpayers are exempt from the mandatory requirement and may use e-invoicing voluntarily. — [Grant Thornton, CREATE MORE e-invoicing](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/tax-notes/create-more-mandatory-use-of-electronic-invoicing-and-electronic-sales-reporting-with-allowable-deduction/); [PwC "Paperless invoicing and sales reporting"](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/taxwise-or-otherwise/2025/paperles-invoicing-and-sales-reporting.html); [Philstar "Keeping up with e-invoicing" (Nov 2025)](https://www.philstar.com/business/2025/11/25/2489594/keeping-e-invoicing)

**BMBE (Barangay Micro Business Enterprise, RA 9178)**
- A BMBE is any business engaged in production, processing, manufacturing, trading, or services whose total assets (including loans, excluding land) do not exceed PHP 3,000,000. Registration is at the DTI Negosyo Center with a copy of the DTI/SEC certificate; approval takes a maximum of 15 days (deemed approved otherwise); registration and the Certificate of Authority are free of charge. — [DTI BMBE FAQ (PDF)](https://esigaw.dti.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/BMBE.pdf); [Rappler](https://www.rappler.com/business/190432-bmbe-benefits-registration-tax-exemption/)
- BMBEs are exempt from income tax on income from operations, but not from VAT or percentage taxes; the Certificate of Authority is presented to the BIR RDO for the exemption to be reflected. — [FAME PH 2025](https://fameph.org/2025/04/09/how-barangay-micro-business-enterprises-bmbe-can-help-you-save-on-taxes/); [Respicio, BIR compliance for BMBE](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/bir-compliance-requirements-for-bmbe-owners)

### Inferences
- For a platform that will hold tens of thousands of USD of other people's money in escrow, the OPC's limited liability is the main reason to prefer it over a sole proprietorship despite the slightly higher cost and annual SEC filings (GIS, audited financial statements). A sole proprietorship exposes the founder's personal assets to creator refund claims, researcher payout claims, and regulatory fines.
- Because an OPC has no general minimum capital, the founder can start an OPC with a modest authorized capital (e.g., PHP 100,000–250,000 — my illustration, not a sourced figure), keeping the SEC filing fee at or near the PHP 2,000 floor.
- If starting as a sole proprietor, choose **National** DTI scope (PHP 2,030) because CRENT is a nationwide online brand and the founder may later want to convert to a corporation or sell.
- Naga City's physical-premises clearances (occupancy, fire, sanitary) still apply to a home-based online business; expect the LGU to assess a business tax on declared capital in year one and on gross receipts thereafter (unverified — from general knowledge of the Local Government Code).
- CRENT clearly falls in RR 11-2025's "engaged in e-commerce or internet transactions" class, so plan for mandatory e-invoicing by 31 Dec 2026 unless CRENT is classified as a "micro" taxpayer (gross sales below PHP 3M under EOPT classification — unverified threshold, from general knowledge).

### Gaps
- Naga City's actual permit fee schedule (mayor's permit fee, business tax rates, zoning/sanitary/fire fees) could not be fetched (naga.gov.ph blocked). Fees are set by the local revenue code and will differ from other LGUs.
- OPC-specific requirements (nominee and alternate nominee, treasurer's bond when the single stockholder is also treasurer, GIS/AFS filing calendar) could not be verified in this session; InCorp/Respicio pages were blocked.
- Whether BMBE registration is available to an online service platform (vs. the professional-services exclusion) was not confirmed; the DTI FAQ was blocked. Also note the 8% income-tax option and BMBE income-tax exemption interact (Respicio has a piece on this: [link](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/eligibility-for-8-tax-rate-as-bmbe-registered-business-in-the-philippines)) but the content was not readable.

## Key Question 2 — Internet Transactions Act (RA 11967) and IRR: obligations for e-marketplaces

### Takeaway
The ITA has been fully enforceable since 20 June 2025 (end of the 18-month transitory period); CRENT is almost certainly an "e-marketplace" because at least one party (the Philippine researchers) is in the Philippines, so it must verify and record its merchants (the researchers), collect their BIR registration, disclose seller and transaction details, run an internal complaints/redress mechanism, act on takedown orders, and register with the DTI E-Commerce Bureau's Online Business Database, with administrative fines reported at up to PHP 1 million (one source says PHP 5 million) plus takedown and blacklisting.

### Cited Findings
- The ITA applies to all B2B and B2C internet transactions within DTI's mandate "where one (1) of the parties is situated in the Philippines or where the digital platform, e-retailer, or online merchant is availing of the Philippine market and has minimum contacts therein"; online media content and C2C transactions are excluded. — [Lexology primer on RA 11967](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=9b9d5ac2-f4a6-49b5-ac49-aed56ffcf7ba); [In-House Community overview](https://www.inhousecommunity.com/article/philippine-internet-transactions-act-overview/)
- The IRR defines "availment of the Philippine market" as any action indicating intention to transact with persons in the Philippines and "minimum contacts" as any touchpoint with an actual or potential customer located in the Philippines. — [Trustiics ITA guide](https://www.trustiics.com/posts/philippines-internet-transactions-act-guide)
- The DTI issued the ITA IRR on 24 May 2024. — [Global Compliance News / Baker McKenzie](https://www.globalcompliancenews.com/2024/10/22/https-insightplus-bakermckenzie-com-bm-consumer-goods-retail_1-philippines-implementing-rules-of-the-internet-transactions-act-issued_10152024/)
- The 18-month transitory period ended on 20 June 2025 and the Act is now in full effect; DTI can now issue takedown orders and platforms may be solidarily liable with sellers if they fail to act on illicit activity. — [Manila Times, 24 June 2025](https://www.manilatimes.net/2025/06/24/business/top-business/internet-transactions-act-now-in-effect/2138101); [eLegal.ph](https://elegal.ph/dti-begins-full-enforceability-of-internet-transactions-act/); [Lexology, transitory period ends](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=6b838df6-5975-48ec-aab8-84a17f994b1a)
- E-marketplaces must ensure all transactions are clearly identified as internet transactions with accurate identification of the parties and promotions, and must collect merchant information such as business name, contact details and proof of identity; they must require online merchants to submit their BIR Certificate of Registration. — [Lexology, "Regulating E-Commerce"](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=906e5cda-b13c-4582-b6f8-0dcf21dd99fd); [Cruz Marcelo](https://cruzmarcelo.com/philippines-internet-transactions-act-now-fully-enforced-what-online-businesses-must-do-to-comply/) (snippets)
- Platforms must exercise ordinary diligence in verifying sellers, remove illegal content within 48 hours of notice, provide accessible dispute resolution, maintain a list of registered merchants, implement an internal redress mechanism for complaints, and comply with the Data Privacy Act. — [Cruz Marcelo](https://cruzmarcelo.com/philippines-internet-transactions-act-now-fully-enforced-what-online-businesses-must-do-to-comply/); [Respicio / lawyer-philippines.com](https://www.lawyer-philippines.com/articles/e-commerce-marketplace-fraud-refund-procedure-philippines)
- All e-marketplaces, e-retailers and online merchants must disclose price, brand name, description, condition and seller contact details for all goods and services; merchants must submit corporate/trade name, business address, contact details and professional accreditation (if applicable) to the DTI E-Commerce Bureau, publish them prominently on their site, and support them with a government-issued ID or business registration. — [Manila Times](https://www.manilatimes.net/2025/06/24/business/top-business/internet-transactions-act-now-in-effect/2138101)
- The E-Commerce Bureau must be established within 6 months of effectivity and must establish an Online Business Database of digital platforms, e-marketplaces, e-retailers and online merchants within one year of effectivity. — [ACCRALAW](https://accralaw.com/2024/01/31/internet-transactions-act-and-philippine-e-commerce/); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=cea260bd-5307-4e0c-896c-9bd0ed91ec62)
- DTI launched the Philippine Online Dispute Resolution System (PODRS) and the Consumer Complaints Assistance and Resolution (CARe) System on 13 October 2023 (www.podrs.dti.gov); DTI E-Commerce Bureau officials describe an ODR platform, the Online Business Database and a One DTI Portal as upcoming systems. — [Manila Bulletin](https://mb.com.ph/2023/10/13/dti-launches-podrs-ca-re-system-for-easier-filing-of-complaints); [Chambers article](https://chambers.com/articles/internet-transactions-act-and-philippine-e-commerce)
- Penalties: DTI may impose administrative fines "of up to PHP 1 million" against merchants, e-retailers, e-marketplaces and platforms, in addition to takedown, without prejudice to civil/criminal liability; the schedule reportedly runs PHP 20,000–100,000 for a first offense and PHP 100,000–500,000 for a second, escalating thereafter. — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=cea260bd-5307-4e0c-896c-9bd0ed91ec62); **contradicted by** [Respicio / lawyer-philippines.com](https://www.lawyer-philippines.com/articles/online-marketplace-scam-complaint-philippines), which states "fines up to ₱5 million for platforms that ignore fraud complaints" and that the ITA "mandates marketplace escrow."
- DTI may blacklist websites, platforms and social-media accounts found repeatedly non-compliant and share the blacklist with payment providers, regulators and the public. — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=cea260bd-5307-4e0c-896c-9bd0ed91ec62)
- Primary text: [RA 11967 on lawphil](https://www.lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2023/ra_11967_2023.html); [Supreme Court E-Library](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/96902) (both blocked in this session).

### Inferences
- CRENT is a B2B platform (creator businesses buying research from Philippine freelancers), and the Philippine researchers are the "online merchants." So CRENT's ITA merchant-verification duties run toward the researchers: collect name, address, contact details, government ID and BIR Certificate of Registration, keep a merchant list, and be ready to furnish it to the E-Commerce Bureau. This dovetails with the platform's own "verified researcher" promise.
- Even though the paying customers are abroad, the extraterritorial "one party in the Philippines" test is satisfied, so the ITA applies; and foreign creators may still raise complaints via PODRS/CARe.
- "Must do before launch": written terms identifying CRENT as an e-marketplace, a visible complaints/redress channel, a 48-hour takedown workflow, researcher KYC + BIR COR collection, and registration in the E-Commerce Bureau database once the registration mechanism is open (status unclear — see Gaps).

### Gaps
- Whether the Online Business Database is actually accepting registrations as of October 2026, what form/fee it uses, and whether it is mandatory for small platforms could not be confirmed; DTI pages were blocked.
- The precise ITA Section 31 fine schedule (first/second/third offense) and whether the top figure is PHP 1M or PHP 5M could not be verified against the statute text (lawphil and e-library blocked). Sources conflict; treat the amounts as approximate.
- The IRR provisions on e-marketplace **escrow or payment-holding** obligations (one source says the ITA "mandates marketplace escrow") could not be verified — this matters directly for CRENT's design and should be checked by counsel against IRR Rule text.
- Whether the ITA's exclusion of "online media content" has any bearing on a platform selling *video ideas/research briefs* is untested; most likely CRENT is a services marketplace, not media content, but a lawyer should confirm.

## Key Question 3 — Data Privacy Act (RA 10173) and NPC

### Takeaway
Every personal information controller must designate a Data Protection Officer and comply with the DPA, but registration of the data processing system with the NPC is mandatory only above thresholds (250+ employees, or sensitive personal information of 1,000+ individuals, or processing likely to pose a risk to data subjects — and always where automated decision-making/profiling is involved); storing government-ID images of researchers is sensitive personal information, so CRENT will cross the mandatory threshold once it holds IDs for roughly 1,000 researchers, and breaches must be reported to the NPC within 72 hours.

### Cited Findings
- NPC Circular 2022-04 mandates registration of Data Processing Systems for PICs/PIPs that employ 250 or more persons, or process sensitive personal information of 1,000 or more individuals, or process data likely to pose a risk to the rights and freedoms of data subjects; those below the thresholds may register voluntarily. — [NPC Circular 2022-04 (PDF)](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Circular-2022-04-1.pdf) (snippet); [Alburo Law](https://www.alburolaw.com/registration-of-data-processing-system-and-designation-of-data-protection-officer-mandated-by-npc-circular-no-2022-04/); [NDV Law CEO's guide](https://ndvlaw.com/a-ceos-guide-to-registration-of-data-processing-system-with-the-national-privacy-commission-part-1-legal-foundations-and-mandatory-requirements/)
- Any system that processes personal or sensitive information involving automated decision-making or profiling must be registered in all instances, regardless of company size or data volume. — [InCorp advisory](https://philippines.incorp.asia/advisories/npc-mandates-businesses-processing-personal-data-to-register/)
- A covered PIC/PIP must register a newly implemented Data Processing System or inaugural DPO within 20 days from commencement of the system or effectivity of the appointment. — [Global Compliance News / Baker McKenzie](https://www.globalcompliancenews.com/2023/01/14/https-insightplus-bakermckenzie-com-bm-technology-media-telecommunications_1-philippines-new-npc-circular-on-registration-of-data-protection-officers-and-data-processing-systems-takes-effect_0111202/)
- Registration of the DPS and DPO with the NPC "is one of the means through which a PIC or PIP demonstrates its compliance with the DPA." — [NPC reminder](https://privacy.gov.ph/reminder-on-mandatory-data-protection-officer-and-data-processing-system-registration/)
- Breach notification: the NPC must be notified within 72 hours upon knowledge of, or reasonable belief that, a personal data breach has occurred; where the breach involves at least 100 data subjects or disclosure of sensitive personal information would harm data subjects, no delay is permitted. The notification form must name the DPO. The PIC remains responsible for notification even when processing is outsourced to a PIP. — [NPC Circular 16-03 (PDF)](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/sgd-npc-circular-16-03-personal-data-breach-management.pdf) (snippet); [NPC breach reporting page](https://privacy.gov.ph/pips-and-pics/breach-reporting/); [CyberCode.ph](https://cybercode.ph/data-breach-notification-philippines-npc/)
- Administrative fines (NPC Circular 2022-01, in force from 27 Aug 2022): grave infractions 0.5%–3% of annual gross income; major infractions 0.25%–2% of annual gross income; total fine capped at PHP 5,000,000; factors include number of data subjects, negligence/intent, categories of data, and duration/severity. — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=abf6d3a3-2040-480a-a011-fa16742d3170); [NPC FAQs on administrative fines (PDF)](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/FAQs-on-the-Guidelines-on-Administrative-Fines-.pdf); [Baker McKenzie handbook](https://resourcehub.bakermckenzie.com/en/resources/global-data-and-cyber-handbook/asia-pacific/philippines/topics/regulators-enforcement-priorities-and-penalties)
- NPC Circular 2024-01 is an amendment to the 2021 NPC Rules of Procedure, not a fines schedule. — [NPC Circular 2024-01 (PDF)](https://privacy.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/NPC-Circular-2024-01-Amendments-to-the-2021-Rules-of-Procedure-of-the-NPC-FOR-PUBLICATION.pdf)

### Inferences
- Government-issued ID numbers/images are "sensitive personal information" under DPA Sec. 3(l) ("issued by government agencies peculiar to an individual") (unverified — from general knowledge of the statute text, which I could not fetch). CRENT's researcher KYC therefore processes SPI from day one; mandatory NPC registration kicks in at 1,000 verified researchers, or immediately if CRENT uses automated scoring/ranking of researchers (profiling).
- Practical design: verify IDs through the licensed payment/KYC provider where possible and retain only a verification result plus minimal metadata, rather than storing ID photos indefinitely; if photos are stored, encrypt at rest, set a retention schedule, and document the lawful basis (consent + legal obligation under ITA/AMLA-related KYC).
- A DPO must be designated regardless of size; for a one-person startup the founder can be the DPO initially (common practice; unverified).
- Cross-border transfers: creator data (US/UK/AU) and researcher data stored on foreign cloud hosts implicate DPA cross-border rules and, for UK/AU users, those countries' laws — flag for counsel.
- Must-do before launch: privacy notice, consent flows, DPO designation, breach response plan (72-hour clock), data-processing agreements with the payment provider/KYC vendor and cloud host.

### Gaps
- NPC registration fee (believed free via the NPC Registration System, NPCRS — unverified) and the registration validity/renewal cycle could not be confirmed.
- Whether a one-person OPC/sole proprietor may self-designate as DPO without conflict, and NPC guidance for micro-enterprises, could not be read (privacy.gov.ph blocked).
- Specific NPC guidance on retention of ID images for KYC was not found.

## Key Question 4 — Holding escrow / money for others: BSP licensing (OPS under RA 11127, EMI) and the safe structure

### Takeaway
Entities that enable sellers to accept payments — payment gateways, platform providers, payment facilitators, merchant acquirers — are "Operators of Payment Systems" that must register with the BSP under Circular 1049/NPSA, and issuing stored value requires an e-money issuer licence; the sources show that Philippine marketplaces avoid these licences by routing collection, holding and split payouts through a BSP-supervised provider (e.g., Xendit's xenPlatform, PayMongo), but no source explicitly confirmed that a marketplace holding client funds in its own account is outside OPS registration, so this is the single most important "ask a lawyer" question.

### Cited Findings
- All OPS must register with the BSP under Circular 1049 (Rules on Registration of Operators of Payment Systems under the NPSA, RA 11127). — [BSP FAQ on OPS registration (PDF)](https://www.bsp.gov.ph/PaymentAndSettlement/FAQ_OPS_Registration.pdf) (snippet); [BSP MORPS (updated Dec 2025)](https://www.bsp.gov.ph/Regulations/MORPS/MORPS.pdf)
- OPS "include cash-in service providers, bills payment service providers, and entities such as payment gateways, platform providers, payment facilitators and merchant acquirers that enable sellers of goods and services to accept payments, in cash or digital form." — [PIA, BSP reminder](https://mirror.pia.gov.ph/press-releases/2021/07/20/bsp-reminds-the-public-to-transact-only-with-registered-operators-of-payment-systems)
- "Any entity offering an e-wallet, payment gateway, remittance service, or even a point-of-sale solution is considered an operator of payment system." — [Loft.ph](https://loft.ph/bsp-operator-of-payment-system/)
- Registration allows BSP oversight "to ensure that it functions safely, efficiently, and reliably ... consistent with the central bank's objectives of consumer protection and financial stability." — [DivinaLaw, Regulating payment systems operators](https://www.divinalaw.com/dose-of-law/regulating-payment-systems-operators/) (snippet)
- E-money issuers are classified as EMI-Bank, EMI-NBFI and EMI-Others; e-money is not a deposit and is not PDIC-insured; BSP lifted its moratorium on new non-bank EMI licences effective 16 December 2024; there are 42 non-bank EMIs including Maya Philippines Inc. and G-Xchange Inc. (GCash). — [Inquirer](https://business.inquirer.net/499980/bsp-to-liberalize-e-money-issuance); [NDV Law on EMIs](https://ndvlaw.com/learning-about-electronic-money-issuers-emi-in-the-philippines/); [Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/business/2022/05/10/2179891/6-non-bank-e-money-issuers-get-bsp-license)
- Xendit Philippines Inc. "is supervised and regulated by the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas"; its xenPlatform product lets marketplace operators onboard sellers/buyers at scale, accept, split and route payments to partners, and run disbursements/batch payouts. — [Xendit marketplace solutions](https://www.xendit.co/en/marketplace-solutions/); [Xendit xenPlatform](https://www.xendit.co/en-ph/products/xenplatform/)
- PayMongo: collected funds sit in a PayMongo balance on a schedule before payout to a nominated Philippine bank account; marketplace splitting may require custom build, whereas Xendit offers disbursements, batch payouts and virtual accounts "ideal for marketplaces." — [Prosperna, PayMongo vs Xendit 2025](https://www.prosperna.com/blog/paymongo-vs-xendit-2025-fees-and-features-for-ph-smes); [Oliver Revelo 2026 comparison](https://www.oliverrevelo.com/blog/payment-gateways-for-filipino-businesses)
- General marketplace mechanics (split payments, escrow-like holds by the PSP) are described at [Sharpay](https://sharpay.net/blog/marketplace-split-payments-escrow) and [Zoho Payments](https://zoho.com/payments/academy/payment-basics/marketplace-payments.html) (non-Philippine, explanatory only).

### Inferences
- The "safe structure" that the sources support: (1) creators pay USD via a licensed acquirer/gateway (e.g., Stripe/PayPal abroad or Xendit/PayMongo card acceptance); (2) funds are held in the BSP-supervised PSP's platform/sub-ledger accounts (xenPlatform-style), not in CRENT's own operating bank account; (3) on milestone approval the PSP splits: CRENT's 5% + 10% fees to CRENT, the remainder disbursed in PHP to the researcher's GCash/Maya/bank via the PSP's disbursement rails. In this model CRENT never "operates" a payment system or issues stored value; it is a merchant/platform client of a registered OPS/EMI.
- If CRENT instead collects creator budgets into its own bank account and pays researchers out of it, it is arguably a "platform provider/payment facilitator that enables sellers to accept payments" — i.e., an OPS requiring BSP registration, and possibly an EMI if balances are stored and withdrawable. This is the structure to avoid without BSP advice.
- The term "escrow" should be avoided in marketing/terms unless counsel confirms CRENT (or the PSP) can lawfully offer it; use "funds held by our payment partner until approval."

### Gaps
- Could not read Circular 1049 or the BSP FAQ to confirm (a) the registration fee, (b) penalties for unregistered OPS, and (c) whether BSP has carved out merchants/marketplaces whose payments are fully processed by a registered OPS. BSP domains were blocked.
- No source was found describing how Shopee/Lazada/Upwork-type platforms are specifically licensed or exempt in the Philippines (e.g., whether ShopeePay/Lazada Wallet hold separate EMI licences — likely yes, unverified).
- Whether USD-denominated collection by a Philippine PSP (and FX conversion to PHP) triggers BSP foreign-exchange reporting for CRENT was not researched.
- Whether CRENT's PSP will accept a sole proprietor vs require a corporation for a platform/split-payment account was not confirmed (commercial question for Xendit/PayMongo onboarding).

## Key Question 5 — Taxes: VAT/percentage tax, RA 12023 digital-services VAT, zero-rating, 8% option, withholding on researcher payouts, RR 16-2023, 1601 forms

### Takeaway
Below PHP 3M gross receipts CRENT is a non-VAT taxpayer paying 3% percentage tax or electing the 8% flat tax on gross receipts (which replaces both income tax and percentage tax for individuals); CRENT's fees charged to foreign creators are a strong zero-rating candidate under NIRC 108(B)(2) once VAT-registered but RA 12023/RR 3-2025 treats digital services as VATable only when the buyer is in the Philippines; the hardest open question is whether CRENT must withhold on researcher payouts — either 5%/10% EWT on professional fees (if CRENT is the "payor") or the 1%-of-half-of-remittances e-marketplace withholding under RR 16-2023 (if CRENT is an "e-marketplace operator" remitting to merchants above PHP 500,000/year).

### Cited Findings

**VAT vs percentage tax; 8% option**
- Section 116 imposes a 3% tax on gross quarterly sales of persons whose sales are VAT-exempt and who are not VAT-registered, with annual gross sales not exceeding PHP 3M; above PHP 3M, VAT applies. The rate is 3% for 2024–2026 (the 1% CREATE-era rate ended). Filed on BIR Form 2551Q, due the 25th day after each quarter. — [CloudCFO](https://cloudcfo.ph/resources/ph-taxes/percentage-tax); [Orkids](https://orkids.ph/guides/percentage-tax-philippines); [Respicio on CREATE rate duration](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/duration-of-reduced-percentage-tax-under-create-law-in-philippines)
- The 8% income tax option is available only to self-employed individuals (sole proprietors/professionals) whose gross sales/receipts and other non-operating income do not exceed PHP 3M and who are not subject to other percentage taxes; it is 8% on gross receipts in excess of PHP 250,000 and is in lieu of both graduated income tax and the 3% percentage tax (RMO 23-2018). — [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/lets-talk-tax/the-8-tax-for-self-employed-individuals/); [BIR RMO 23-2018 digest (PDF)](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/local/pdf/RMO_No.%2023-2018_digest.pdf); [Beppo](https://beppo.com/blog/8-income-tax-rate-option-for-freelancers)
- The 8% option is for individuals; a corporation (including an OPC) cannot use it (implicit in the sources above, which limit it to "self-employed individuals"). Corporate income tax rates were not researched (see Gaps).

**RA 12023 (VAT on Digital Services) and RR 3-2025**
- RA 12023 was signed 2 October 2024; RR 3-2025 (16 Jan 2025) implements it, imposing 12% VAT on digital services consumed in the Philippines, affecting both resident and nonresident DSPs. — [Law.asia](https://law.asia/philippines-vat-digital-services-ra-12023-update/); [PwC Tax Alert 3-2025](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/tax-alerts/2025/tax-alert-3.html); [BIR RR 3-2025 (PDF)](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%203-2025.pdf)
- "Digital services" are services delivered over the internet or electronic networks using IT and predominantly automated (cloud storage, online ads, e-learning, digital content, online marketplaces, etc.). — [Taxumo](https://www.taxumo.com/blog/understanding-bir-revenue-regulations-3-2025-and-14-2025-vat-on-digital-services-in-the-philippines/); [UNA Accounting](https://una-acctg.com/bir-rr-3-2025-vat-digital-services/)
- Digital services are considered performed/consumed in the Philippines, and thus subject to 12% VAT, "if the buyer or user is located in the Philippines, regardless of residence of the digital service provider." — [MTF Counsel](https://mtfcounsel.com/2025/02/14/guidelines-in-imposing-vat-on-digital-services/); [Babylon2k](https://babylon2k.org/rr-no-03-2025-a-game-changer-for-vat-on-digital-services-in-the-philippines/)
- Resident VAT-registered DSPs classified as e-marketplaces with **nonresident** participating sellers must file and pay the VAT due on those sellers' sales; invoices must break down sales by VAT status (12%, zero-rated, exempt). — [PwC](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/tax-alerts/2025/tax-alert-3.html); [Fiscal Solutions](https://www.fiscal-requirements.com/news/3391-the-philippines-enacted-republic-act-no-12023-commonly-known-as-the-vat-on-digital-services-law)
- Exempt digital services include educational services by accredited institutions and services by banks/financial institutions. — [Grant Thornton update](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2025/updates-and-clarifications-on-the-implementation-of-vat-on-digital-services/)

**Zero-rating of services to nonresidents (NIRC 108(B)(2))**
- Conditions for zero-rating: (1) services rendered in the Philippines; (2) paid in acceptable foreign currency accounted for under BSP rules; (3) recipient is a foreign corporation doing business outside the Philippines, or a nonresident person not engaged in business who is outside the Philippines when the services are performed. — [Tax and Accounting Center](https://taxacctgcenter.ph/bir-vat-zero-rating-under-section-108b2-nirc-on-ph-bpos-and-service-exporters/); [PwC "Not as simple as it seems"](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/taxwise-or-otherwise/2024/not-as-simple-as-it-seems.html)
- Proof of nonresidency typically includes an SEC Certificate of Non-Registration and proof of the client's incorporation abroad. — [MTF Counsel, proving nonresident status](https://mtfcounsel.com/2023/04/27/vat-zero-rating/); [UpCloud Accounting](https://go.upcloudaccounting.com/post/zero-rated-vat-for-non-resident-clients-in-the-philippines)
- RR 10-2025 provides updated guidance on these provisions (content not readable). — [Reyes Tacandong](https://www.reyestacandong.com/bir-issuances-rr-no-10-2025/)

**Withholding on payouts to researchers (EWT on professional fees)**
- Professional, talent and commission fees paid to individual payees are subject to 5% EWT if the payee's gross income for the year does not exceed PHP 3M, or 10% if it exceeds PHP 3M; the 5% rate requires the payee's sworn declaration of gross receipts (due 15 January each year or before the first payment) plus a copy of the payee's BIR COR showing non-VAT status; absent the sworn declaration the agent must withhold 10%. — [Grant Thornton alert](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/alerts-and-publications/technical-alerts/tax-alert/2018/additional-condition-for-5-ewt-on-professional-fees-extended-deadlines-for-sworn-declarations/); [Taxify.ph 2026 guide](https://taxify.ph/blog/withholding-tax-professional-fees-philippines/); [Respicio EWT guide](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/guide-to-expanded-withholding-tax-rates-on-professional-fees-and-commissions)
- Freelancers (writers, designers, developers, consultants) are within the professional-fee EWT category. — [Taxify.ph](https://taxify.ph/blog/withholding-tax-professional-fees-philippines/)
- "All taxpayers engaged in doing business or self-employment are mandated by the BIR to act as withholding agents." Forms: 0619-E monthly remittance (first two months of each quarter, due the 10th of the following month); 1601-EQ quarterly return; 1604-E annual information return/alphalist; 2307 certificate to payee. — [MPM.ph 1601-EQ](https://mpm.ph/bir-form-1601eq/); [MPM.ph 0619-E](https://mpm.ph/bir-form-0619e/); [Juan.tax 1604-E](https://juan.tax/form-1604e/); [Numbers That Matter](https://www.numbersthatmatterph.com/post/simplifying-taxation-for-msmes-file-your-bir-form-0619e-and-1601eq-even-without-an-accountant)

**RR 16-2023 (1% withholding by e-marketplace operators / DFSPs)**
- RR 16-2023 (issued 21 Dec 2023, published 27 Dec 2023, effective 11 Jan 2024) requires e-marketplace operators and Digital Financial Services Providers to withhold 1% income tax on one-half of the gross remittances to sellers/merchants for goods and services sold through the platform. — [Triple i Consulting](https://www.tripleiconsulting.com/what-philippine-rr-16-2023-withholding-tax-online-sellers/); [Cruz Marcelo](https://cruzmarcelo.com/quick-look-bir-revenue-regulations-no-16-2023-requires-e-marketplaces-and-digital-financial-services-providers-to-withhold-1-income-tax-on-remittances-to-merchants/); [KPMG copy of RR 16-2023 (PDF)](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ph/pdf/InTAX/2024/RR%20No.%2016-2023.pdf)
- The withholding does not apply if annual total gross remittances to a seller for the past taxable year did not exceed PHP 500,000 (and cumulative remittances in the current year have not exceeded PHP 500,000). The tax is creditable; operators must issue BIR Form 2307. — [Triple i Consulting](https://www.tripleiconsulting.com/what-philippine-rr-16-2023-withholding-tax-online-sellers/); [Cruz Marcelo](https://cruzmarcelo.com/quick-look-bir-revenue-regulations-no-16-2023-requires-e-marketplaces-and-digital-financial-services-providers-to-withhold-1-income-tax-on-remittances-to-merchants/)
- RMC 8-2024 (15 Jan 2024) gave a 90-day transitory period and clarified that where payments pass through several channels (e-wallets, cards, cash), "the last facility in control of payment before full remittance to the seller/merchant bears the tax withholding responsibility." Enforcement started 15 July 2024. — [PwC Tax Alert 5-2024](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/tax-alerts/2024/tax-alert-05.html); [GMA News](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/companies/913492/bir-withholding-tax-on-online-sellers-started-july-15/story/); [Lexology / Baker McKenzie](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=e71879ea-dc34-4c0b-9b96-898e521e942a)
- RMC 79-2024 further clarifies the rules for online sellers. — [AccountItRight](https://accountitright.com/rmc-no-79-2024-new-tax-rules-for-online-sellers/) (content not read)

### Inferences
- **Which withholding regime applies to CRENT's payouts is genuinely unsettled** and is the top tax question for counsel. Two readings: (a) CRENT is an *e-marketplace operator* under RR 16-2023 → withhold 1% of 50% of gross remittances only for researchers who exceed PHP 500,000/year, issue 2307s, and do NOT apply 5%/10% EWT (the payor is the creator, not CRENT); (b) CRENT is the *payor of professional fees* (because it contracts with researchers and pays them from funds it controls) → withhold 5%/10% EWT on every payout, collect sworn declarations and CORs. Reading (a) is more consistent with a pure-marketplace structure where the licensed PSP holds funds and CRENT only earns fees; reading (b) becomes more likely if CRENT invoices creators for the full budget and books researcher payments as its own cost. RMC 8-2024's "last facility in control of payment" rule could even put the obligation on the PSP (DFSP) rather than CRENT — ask the PSP how they handle RR 16-2023 for platform clients.
- **VAT on fees to foreign creators:** while CRENT is below PHP 3M it is non-VAT and pays 3% percentage tax (or elects 8%) on its *fees* — not on the full creator budget that passes through, provided the accounting and contracts clearly treat the budget as held for the researcher (gross-receipts definition for pass-through amounts should be confirmed by a CPA). Once VAT-registered, CRENT's fees to foreign businesses paid in USD are a strong candidate for 0% under 108(B)(2) (foreign-currency payment via BSP-compliant channel + foreign client not doing business in PH); RA 12023's "buyer located in the Philippines" test points the same way (foreign buyer → not consumed in PH → not 12%). Fees charged to Philippine researchers (the 10% researcher fee) are domestic services and would be 12% VAT once CRENT is VAT-registered.
- **Structure choice affects tax:** an OPC pays corporate income tax (rates not researched here) and cannot use the 8% option; a sole proprietor can elect 8% on gross receipts above PHP 250,000 — likely cheaper in the first years if net margins are high. This is a real trade-off with the liability point in Key Question 1.
- **Onboarding checklist for researchers (tax):** collect TIN, BIR COR (also required by the ITA), and — if CRENT ends up under the EWT regime — annual sworn declaration of gross receipts (Annex B-1 of RR 11-2018; form name unverified) to apply 5% instead of 10%.

### Gaps
- The exact definition of "e-marketplace" in RR 16-2023 (whether a services/freelance marketplace is squarely covered) could not be read; snippets say "goods and services" generically.
- Whether RR 16-2023's 1% regime *displaces* the 5%/10% professional-fee EWT, or both apply, was not addressed by any source found.
- RR 10-2025 (zero-rating documentation) and RR 3-2025 full text could not be read; whether RA 12023 changed the documentary requirements for zero-rated exported services is unconfirmed.
- Corporate income tax rate for an OPC (believed 20%/25% under CREATE depending on income/assets — unverified) was not researched.
- RMC 55-2013 and RMC 97-2021 (online-seller registration obligations) were not reachable; they are older guidance and largely superseded by RR 16-2023/RR 7-2024 for present purposes.
- The EOPT "micro/small/medium/large" taxpayer classification thresholds (RR 8-2024) were not verified; they determine e-invoicing exemption and some penalty reductions.

## Key Question 6 — Freelancer status of researchers; the (pending) Freelance Workers Protection Act; written contracts

### Takeaway
The Freelance Workers Protection Act is still a pending bill — RA 12063 is the EBET Framework Act, so the task premise was mistaken — but the pending versions would require any "hiring party" to sign a plain-language written contract before work starts (services, compensation and payment schedule, period, breach grounds, TIN), pay within 15 days of the agreed date and (in some versions) 30% upfront, which CRENT should build into its brief/contract flow now because it will either apply directly (if CRENT is deemed the hiring party) or to the creators (if CRENT is a marketplace), and because DOLE will enforce it.

### Cited Findings
- A Supreme Court E-Library entry lists "IRR of Republic Act No. 12063 ... known as the 'Enterprise-Based Education and Training (EBET) Framework Act'" (IRR issued 28 Feb 2025). — [Supreme Court E-Library](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/99251); statute page: [lawphil RA 12063](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2024/ra_12063_2024.html) (blocked; title not independently confirmed)
- The House approved the Freelance Workers Protection Act on third reading; the bill "will be transmitted to the Senate for further deliberation before it can be submitted to the President." — [PNA, House approves](https://www.pna.gov.ph/articles/1134945); [PNA, bill protecting freelance workers](https://www.pna.gov.ph/articles/1194155)
- Senate versions (SB 2107, SB 2006 by Sen. Ejercito requiring a physical contract, SB 1998 by Sen. Lapid) underwent joint committee hearings on 20 August 2024; earlier Villanueva versions were SB 1810 and SB 136. — [L&E Global, Looking Ahead 2025](https://leglobal.law/countries/philippines/looking-ahead-2025-philippines/); [GMA News](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/754749/bill-seeking-protection-for-freelancers-reaches-senate-floor/story/); [Senate LDR subject page](https://ldr.senate.gov.ph/subject/freelance-workers-protection)
- A December 2025 article reports a *new* bill "seeking to protect the rights of freelancers, virtual assistants and riders" being filed in Congress — consistent with the measure not yet being law. — [Cosmo.ph, 15 Dec 2025](https://www.cosmo.ph/lifestyle/career-money/bill-protect-rights-of-freelancers-riders-filed-in-congress-a6906-20251215-dyn)
- Provisions in the House-passed text: any hiring party must execute a written contract before services are rendered, in plain language, with (a) itemization of services, (b) compensation details including rate, method and schedule of payment, (c) period, (d) grounds for breach by either side, (e) other terms DOLE may direct; the contract must state the freelancer's name, address and TIN; each party keeps a signed copy; modifications are unenforceable unless signed by both. — [House Bill 9768 text (PDF)](https://docs.congress.hrep.online/legisdocs/basic_20/HB09768.pdf); [Sprout.ph](https://sprout.ph/articles/5-ways-hr-analytics-dei-philippines-2/); [GA Consulting copy (PDF)](https://thegaconsulting.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Freelance-Workers-Protection-Act.pdf)
- Payment rules in the pending versions: minimum 30% upfront on engagement/signing; payment no later than 15 days after the agreed payment date; DOLE to enforce, investigate and mediate. — [L&E Global](https://leglobal.law/countries/philippines/looking-ahead-2025-philippines/); [HRM Asia](https://hrmasia.com/bill-to-protect-freelancers-in-the-philippines-fast-tracked-for-approval/)
- Villanueva's version defines a freelancer as a person who renders work "through his or her freely chosen means and methods, free from any forms of economic dependence, control or supervision by the clients." — [Rappler](https://www.rappler.com/philippines/senate-takes-up-bill-protecting-freelancers/)

### Inferences
- Today, researcher–creator relationships are ordinary service contracts under the Civil Code; the main present-day risk is not the pending Act but *misclassification*: if CRENT sets rates, assigns work, supervises methods and pays researchers from its own funds, DOLE/NLRC could view CRENT as the employer (four-fold test — unverified, general knowledge). Keep researchers free to choose briefs, set their own pitches/prices, and work by their own methods.
- Design the platform so that every accepted pitch generates a written, timestamped, e-signed contract between creator and researcher that already contains the pending Act's minimum content (scope, price, payment schedule, period, breach grounds, researcher TIN). Escrow funding of the full budget at acceptance more than satisfies the proposed 30%-upfront rule; release within the proposed 15-day window after approval.
- Since the bill lapsed with the 19th Congress and has been refiled in the 20th, its final text (and whether online platforms are expressly treated as "hiring parties") remains unknown — monitor.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm the precise current bill numbers in the 20th Congress or any committee action in 2026.
- Whether the RA 12063/EBET Act has any relevance to CRENT (it concerns enterprise-based training/apprenticeship) was not examined; it appears irrelevant.

## Key Question 7 — Consumer Act / refund policy, terms-of-service essentials, non-circumvention enforceability, IP in "ideas"

### Takeaway
"No return, no exchange"-style blanket no-refund terms are unlawful under the Consumer Act (fines reported up to PHP 300,000), so CRENT's terms must offer remedies for non-delivery or defective work while lawfully excluding change-of-mind refunds; non-circumvention clauses are enforceable under Civil Code Art. 1306 only if reasonable in time, scope and place; and because ideas are expressly not copyrightable under IP Code Sec. 175, protection of researchers' pitches must come from contract (confidentiality, licence-on-payment) rather than copyright.

### Cited Findings
- "No Return, No Exchange" signs/terms have been illegal for over three decades; the Consumer Act (RA 7394) treats them as a deceptive sales act, with administrative fines "of up to PHP 300,000"; sellers must honor warranties with repair, replacement or refund for defects. — [TermsBox](https://termsbox.com/blog/no-return-no-exchange-policy-dti); [Respicio](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/understanding-the-no-return-no-exchange-policy-under-the-consumer-act)
- A refusal to refund is lawful for pure buyer's remorse where the item is free from defects and matches its description; a compliant policy states conditions (e.g., "returns accepted within X days for defective items"). — [TermsBox](https://termsbox.com/blog/no-return-no-exchange-policy-dti)
- The ITA additionally requires platforms to run an internal redress mechanism and accessible dispute resolution, and to disclose price, description and seller contact details for services. — [Manila Times](https://www.manilatimes.net/2025/06/24/business/top-business/internet-transactions-act-now-in-effect/2138101); [Cruz Marcelo](https://cruzmarcelo.com/philippines-internet-transactions-act-now-fully-enforced-what-online-businesses-must-do-to-comply/)
- Civil Code Art. 1306: parties may establish stipulations "provided they are not contrary to law, morals, good customs, public order, or public policy"; Philippine courts uphold restraint clauses (non-compete/non-involvement) only if reasonable as to time, trade and place, weighing legitimate business interest, undue burden, public welfare, and reasonableness of time/territory; the party enforcing bears the burden. — [Legal500 on non-competes](https://www.legal500.com/fivehundred-magazine/employment/non-compete-covenants-in-philippine-employment-agreements-current-views-and-future-direction/); [Philippine Law Journal (Villena)](https://philippinelawjournal.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/83PLJ419_VILLENA.pdf); [Respicio, non-compete law](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/non-compete-law-in-the-philippines)
- IP Code (RA 8293) Sec. 175: no copyright protection extends to "any idea, procedure, system, method or operation, concept, principle, discovery or mere data as such, even if they are expressed, explained, illustrated or embodied in a work," nor to news of the day; only the original expression is protected. — [NDV Law](https://ndvlaw.com/what-subject-matter-is-not-protected-by-copyright/); [Respicio bar notes](https://www.respicio.ph/bar/2025/mercantile-and-taxation-laws/intellectual-property/copyrights/non-copyrightable-works); [IPOPHL Copyright Reference Book 2021 (PDF)](https://ilaw.ipophil.gov.ph/pluginfile.php/150/mod_resource/content/2/Copyright%20Reference%20Book%202021.pdf)

### Inferences
- Terms-of-service essentials for CRENT (synthesised from the above): identify CRENT as an e-marketplace/intermediary, not a party to the research contract; describe the fund-hold and release mechanics and the PSP's role; a refund/dispute policy that (i) refunds the creator in full if no pitch is accepted or the researcher fails to deliver, (ii) provides a revision/partial-refund remedy for non-conforming deliverables, (iii) lawfully excludes change-of-mind refunds after approval; a complaints channel with response SLAs and a 48-hour takedown process; a non-circumvention clause limited to, say, 12 months and to researcher–creator pairs introduced on the platform, with liquidated damages equal to the platform fee (illustrative, not sourced); privacy notice; governing law (Philippines) with ODR/DTI mediation first.
- IP: because a pitch's *idea* cannot be owned, the terms should (a) treat the written pitch/brief response as confidential information of the researcher until purchased; (b) prohibit creators from using unpurchased pitches (contractual, with a liquidated-damages clause); (c) on payment, assign or licence the written deliverable (the expression) to the creator and waive moral rights to the extent allowed (moral-rights waiver limits in PH not verified).
- Non-circumvention clauses that bar a researcher from ever working with a creator met on CRENT would likely fail the reasonableness test; limit in time and tie to the specific introduced relationship.

### Gaps
- No Philippine source specifically on non-circumvention clauses for marketplaces was found; the analysis is extrapolated from non-compete jurisprudence.
- Whether the Consumer Act applies when the "consumer" is a foreign business (B2B) — probably not, since RA 7394 protects natural-person consumers (unverified) — needs counsel; the ITA's redress rules apply regardless.
- Whether Philippine courts would enforce a liquidated-damages clause for using an unpurchased idea was not researched.

## Key Question 8 — Anti-Money Laundering (AMLA) KYC expectations for payouts

### Takeaway
AMLA "covered persons" are financial institutions — banks, e-money issuers (GCash, Maya), payment system operators, remittance agents, etc. — not ordinary marketplaces; CRENT itself is not a covered person if it stays outside OPS/EMI territory, but its licensed PSP and the payout wallets are, so CRENT's practical obligation is to collect enough verified identity data (ID, TIN, matching wallet/bank name) to satisfy the PSP's onboarding and to avoid facilitating structuring, and covered institutions may rely on each other's customer due diligence.

### Cited Findings
- Covered persons include e-wallet providers, fintech platforms handling payments or transfers, VASPs, banks, insurers, securities dealers, remittance companies, pawnshops; they must register with the AMLC, report covered and suspicious transactions and comply with KYC/CDD. — [Sumsub 2026 guide](https://sumsub.com/blog/aml-kyc-in-the-philippines/); [Verihubs AMLA guide](https://verihubs.com/ph/blog/amla-philippines-guide); [Tookitaki](https://www.tookitaki.com/compliance-hub/what-are-covered-transactions-in-amla-a-comprehensive-guide)
- "E-money issuers, payment system operators, remittance companies, pawnshops, insurers, and securities dealers are all covered persons under the AMLA framework." — [Verihubs](https://verihubs.com/ph/blog/amlc-anti-money-laundering-council-philippines)
- For bank or e-wallet payouts, the recipient's account or wallet is already KYC-verified under the same rules; Philippine regulation allows a covered institution to rely on CDD performed by another covered institution provided it can obtain the identification information immediately and remains responsible. — [Zolt Money](https://zoltmoney.com/blogs/amla-amlc-anti-money-laundering-rules-remittances/); [Sumsub](https://sumsub.com/blog/aml-kyc-in-the-philippines/)
- GCash (G-Xchange) and Maya are BSP-licensed EMIs (and therefore AMLA covered persons). — [Inquirer](https://business.inquirer.net/499980/bsp-to-liberalize-e-money-issuance)

### Inferences
- CRENT should: verify each researcher's government ID and TIN; require that the payout GCash/Maya/bank account be in the researcher's own name; keep transaction records (the ITA and tax rules independently require this); and watch for red flags (many accounts to one wallet, rapid refunds). It should not describe itself as doing "AML compliance" as a covered person.
- If CRENT ever holds funds itself or offers wallets/balances, it crosses into OPS/EMI (and AMLA covered-person) territory — another reason for the PSP-held-funds structure.

### Gaps
- The AMLA covered-transaction threshold (PHP 500,000 in one day — general knowledge, unverified) and record-retention period could not be verified from the blocked sources.
- No source addressed whether a *platform* that merely instructs a PSP to disburse is treated as an AMLA "remittance agent"; counsel should confirm with reference to the PSP's terms.

## Key Question 9 — Trade name and trademark: IPOPHL filing for "CRENT" (Classes 35/42); DTI business name vs brand

### Takeaway
Filing "CRENT" with IPOPHL as a small entity costs about PHP 1,200 per class in filing fees (so roughly PHP 2,400 for Classes 35 and 42) plus roughly PHP 900 publication and PHP 570 issuance, with Declarations of Actual Use due in the 3rd year (PHP 900/class) and 5th year (PHP 1,100/class); the DTI business name need not match the brand, but registering "CRENT" (or "CRENT Research Services") as the DTI/SEC name is cheap insurance against a conflicting registration.

### Cited Findings
- IPOPHL trademark filing fee: PHP 1,200 per class for small entities (assets of PHP 100M or less); big entities pay more. — [IPOPHL trademark fees](https://www.ipophil.gov.ph/services/schedule-of-fees/trademark-related-fees/) (snippet); [Triple i Consulting](https://www.tripleiconsulting.com/trademark-registration-philippines-guide-requirements-process-fees/)
- Small-entity fees reported: filing PHP 1,200/class; publication PHP 900; issuance of certificate PHP 570; claim of colour PHP 280/class; 3rd-year DAU PHP 900/class; 5th-year DAU PHP 1,100/class. — [Scribd copy of IPOPHL fee schedule](https://www.scribd.com/document/649505410/IPOPHL-Schedule-of-Trademark-related-fees); [IPOPHL trademark maintenance](https://www.ipophil.gov.ph/trademark/trademark-maintenance/) (snippets; exact current amounts should be re-checked on ipophil.gov.ph)
- The DTI-registered business name (legal name) may differ from the brand/trade name; the brand is protected separately by an IPOPHL trademark. — [FilipiKnow](https://filipiknow.net/dti-online-business-name-registration/); [The Business Manual](https://thebusinessmanual.ph/business-101/sme-basics/business-name-registration-online-dti/)
- DTI business-name registration is valid for 5 years; national scope costs PHP 2,030 with DST. — [Orkids](https://orkids.ph/guides/dti-business-name-registration); [Pinoy Negosyo](https://pinoynegosyo.net/dti-business-registration-3912.html)

### Inferences
- Budget roughly PHP 4,000–5,000 in IPOPHL government fees for a two-class word mark filed online via eTMfile, before any agent fees; timeline to registration is typically 6–12 months absent oppositions (unverified — from general knowledge).
- Class 35 (business/marketplace/advertising services) and Class 42 (SaaS/platform) are the right classes; consider Class 41 if "research/content ideas" is treated as educational/entertainment content (judgement call for an IP agent).
- Run an IPOPHL trademark search for "CRENT" and near-identical marks (e.g., "Crent", "Krent") before DTI/SEC name filing to avoid an objection or later rebrand; DTI/SEC name approval does not confer trademark rights.
- Foreign protection (US/UK/AU where customers are) is a later step via Madrid Protocol through IPOPHL (unverified detail; the Philippines is a Madrid member — general knowledge).

- Processing timelines and the eTMfile procedure were not verifiable in this session.
